## Native status
- **California native, and endemic to California** (Calflora: "native to California, and endemic (limited) to California").
- **Wild only on the southern Channel Islands. Confirmed.** FNA lists 3 varieties, all insular:
  - var. giganteum: Santa Catalina Island
  - var. compactum: Santa Barbara and Sutil islands; rare, cultivated only in botanical gardens
  - var. formosum: San Clemente Island

  Jepson (var. giganteum): "s ChI (Santa Catalina Island); widely cult, occasionally established CW, SW".
- **Not native to the mainland or the Inland Empire.** Mainland plants are garden or roadside plantings, sometimes naturalized.
  - iNaturalist has 18 observations in Riverside and San Bernardino counties: Fontana, Chino and Redlands, all in town or by roads. These are plantings, even though most aren't flagged "captive".
  - Caveat: FNA and Jepson note that it **hybridizes with California buckwheat (E. fasciculatum)** and E. arborescens where planted on the mainland. FNA even urges removing it from coastal California. Calscape: don't plant it next to wildlands. For an IE nursery, this means suggesting it for gardens, not for restoration plantings next to open land. Eric's call whether to add a note.

## How it does in Inland Empire gardens
- **Heat:** good. Calscape: "adapts to high inland temperatures". The Waterwise Garden Planner (IE-specific): "adapts well to all parts of the Inland Empire". UCCE: "easily adapts to summer temperatures in the Valley". Set `ie:true` (Heat tough chip).
- **Frost:** the sources disagree.
  - Calscape and UCCE: tolerates cold to 15–25 °F.
  - Theodore Payne: "frost tender" / "cold hardy to 32 °F".
  - Calscape text also says it "does not do well in frost".

  IE valleys usually stay above about 25 °F, but cold pockets and foothills can drop lower. Young plants may need frost cloth on hard-freeze nights. **Eric 2026-10-25: no frost note on the card.**
